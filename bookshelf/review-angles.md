# Review angles

The master list for `carousel-critique` and `carousel-review`. A critique round declares one angle from this list, by name, and uses a different one each round. The last round of a plan with two or more rounds takes angle 16. Names here are the names to write in the round header.

### 1. Literal ask

Is the question the client asked answered on slide 1 or 2, in our words? Would the person who asked recognize their question and find the answer?

### 2. Headlines only

Read the headlines in order and nothing else. Is there a beginning, a turn and an answer? Which headline could swap places with another without anyone noticing?

### 3. One window, one source

Does every number come from one window and one source family? Find any slide that quietly uses a different date range, population or data pull.

### 4. Story sequence

Does the order run from the widest scope to the narrowest, or as the brief says? Which slide would break the argument if removed, and which would not?

### 5. Recurrence

When you find a flaw, check the slides built from the same template or source for it.

### 6. Ledger integrity

Did a fix change a number, a source or a window and leave its ledger row stale? Re-read every row against the current slide text.

### 7. Slide-to-slide consistency

Pick each convention (scope tag, source line, base, spelling) and find the first slide that drops it.

### 8. Reader and venue

Who reads this, and where: a screen share, an emailed file, a printout, a room? Does each slide survive that setting at that size?

### 9. Skeptical executive

Write the three hardest questions a doubtful senior reader would ask. Where in the deck, or its appendix, is each answered?

### 10. The unreconcilable number

Pick the five most load-bearing numbers. Rebuild each from its source from scratch. Which one does not come out the same?

### 11. Counter-evidence

What is the strongest fact against the main claim, and where does the deck show it?

### 12. Causal overreach

Find every verb that implies cause. Does the evidence support cause, or only a pattern? Reword to "worth testing" where it does not.

### 13. Quote and name fidelity

Is every quote exact, with attribution? Is every name, program and segment spelled as the source spells it?

### 14. Length and cuts

Is the deck within the budget? If you had to cut two slides, which, and what does the argument lose?

### 15. Denominators

Does each percentage show its base? Does any chart compare things measured on different bases?

### 16. Open rows

Last round of a plan with two or more rounds. Review every row on an earlier `Open rows:` line. Resolve each to checked evidence, reword it to "worth testing", or cut it.

## Choosing an angle

Pick the angle most likely to find something given what earlier rounds did not look at. After a round that found a number problem, angle 6 or 10 follows naturally. After a round that reordered slides, angle 2 checks the new order. With one round on the short run, take angle 10, and resolve anything the round parks before it ends.
