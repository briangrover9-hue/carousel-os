# The writing standard

Every word on every slide, in every summary, memo, and delivery message follows this. These are house editorial defaults. Follow explicit wording or style requests from the person; accuracy, verbatim quotes and confidentiality remain mandatory. Unrequested violations are corrected before delivery. The goal is prose that sounds like a person who chose every word, addressed to a busy executive.

## Hard bans (find-check before delivering, literally search the text)

These forms are banned. They are quoted here as examples, so the block is fenced.

```
No em dashes, en dashes, or spaced hyphens as punctuation. Use commas, colons, periods, "to" for ranges, "+" for combos. Standard compound hyphens (better-for-you) are fine.
No negation-reframe ("it was never about X, it was about Y"; "not X but Y").
No fragment stacking (runs of short 3-6 word declaratives used as a drumbeat).
No rule-of-three triads used for rhythm.
No grand closing pronouncements ("this is just the beginning").
No prophecy cadence ("this will feel slow, and then it won't").
No "isn't just X, it's Y" escalation.
No slop vocabulary: best-in-class, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, world-class, game changer, unlock, dive in, delve, journey, landscape (as metaphor), leverage (as verb), utilize, synergy, holistic, impactful, transformative, learnings.
```

## The six rules (Orwell, applied as procedure)

1. Never use a metaphor, simile, or figure of speech you are used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word, or a jargon word if an everyday English equivalent exists.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

Before drafting, answer in order: What am I trying to say? What words will express it? What image or example makes it clearer? Could I put it more shortly? Bad prose comes from letting ready-made phrases think for you.

## The four swindles to scan for

```
1. **Dying metaphors:** low-hanging fruit, move the needle, north star, table stakes, paradigm shift, at the end of the day, tip of the iceberg. Fix: say the plain thing.
2. **Verbal false limbs:** utilize (use), leverage (use), facilitate (help), in order to (to), due to the fact that (because), it should be noted that (delete), is able to (can), going forward (delete). Fix: find the buried verb and let it work.
3. **Pretentious diction:** methodology (method), holistic, ecosystem (unless literal), operationalize, ideate. Fix: translate into what you would say out loud to a colleague. If the plain version sounds too small, the problem is the claim, not the vocabulary.
4. **Meaningless praise words:** innovative, powerful, compelling, scalable-as-praise. Fix: replace the adjective with the fact that earned it; "a robust pipeline" becomes "the pipeline retries failed batches and alerts after three failures". If no fact earned it, cut the claim.
```
5. **Mannered prose:** metaphor and flourish substituting for direct statement, even when the metaphor is fresh. "A dial worth turning" for "a parameter worth varying"; "this point earns its keep" for "this point still matters". These phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell; they also drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control. Fix: say what you mean; when a literal phrase is available, use it. (This extends Orwell rule 1, which catches only metaphors already seen in print; a metaphor being original does not excuse it.) This is a judgment scan, not a string search: run it during the read-aloud pass.

## The editing passes (run on all deliverable prose before review)

1. **Meaning:** each paragraph or slide body in one plain sentence; if you cannot say it, it does not know either.
2. **Cut:** throat-clearing openers, doubled modifiers, restatements. Most drafts lose 20-30% here.
3. **Verbs:** hunt passives, name the actor, restore buried verbs.
4. **Swindle scan:** lists 1 to 4 above plus the hard bans as literal text searches; swindle 5 (mannered prose) by judgment during pass 5.
5. **Read aloud:** anything you would not say out loud to a colleague gets rewritten until you would.

## Slide-specific rules

- Headlines state the conclusion, never label the topic. Varied grammar across the deck: no repeated antithesis template, no template of any kind repeated slide after slide.
- Write the finding the reader will repeat in their own meeting (the soundbite). The ones that travel put a number and its contrast in one line ("down 5% while the rest grew 10%", each with its base on the slide per preflight item 15; "from 4.2 to 2.6 stars in four weeks", "five times the category rate"), state a zero with its base ("none of 7,000 reviews mention it"), set a time box, or settle the reader's own hypothesis. They are almost never a recommendation. A zero ships only with its base and a coverage check (preflight items 21 and 22).
- Body sentences cap at ~35 words; re-check after ANY text sweep, because inserting one word can push an at-limit sentence over.
- Vocabulary the audience uses daily. For CPG insights audiences: territory, segment, fair share, share of growth, velocity, distribution. Never analyst-internal shorthand as slide language.
- Causal wording only where evidence supports it; otherwise test-framing ("the most promising combination to test"), never a directive. Prevalence claims the data fully supports stay punchy: keep the punch the evidence earns.
- Numbers in prose follow the tiering rules (billions at or above $1,000M, one decimal).

## Reader-facing hygiene (anything the audience will see)

- Never expose internal tool, pipeline, tenant, or system names in headlines or body text; describe the capability ("our product enrichment", "review analysis across N retailers"), not the plumbing. The one exception is the source line, which names the data system, the market and the window.
- Never identify a consumer: quotes carry platform/retailer, rating or engagement, and date, never a username or handle.
- Anonymize customer names unless a published reference already names them.
- Translate internal jargon before it ships: segment predicates become plain descriptions, field names become English, and any term the audience does not use daily gets replaced or defined on first use.

## Readability target

Slide body text and summary prose aim at reading grade 9-12: short sentences, one clause doing the work. Scan for offenders mechanically: sentences over ~35 words, comma pile-ups (3+), and passives; rewrite rather than trim mid-clause.

## What editing never touches

Verbatim quotes, legal text, names and taxonomy, code, precise technical terms where the everyday word loses precision. Editing tightens how a fact is said, never changes the fact. If a claim only survives in vague wording, flag it, do not launder it.
