# Narrative devices: named slide beats and analytical framings

A library of named moves for `carousel-storyboard`. These are candidates, never a template: pick the few the evidence earns, and never run the same beat twice in a row. Each beat carries across topics and segmentation choices, so you can borrow one from a slide plan that lost and use it in the one that won.

## Slide beats

1. **The shift**: the one-line change in the world that makes this deck timely. Opens a deck when the evidence supports urgency.
2. **Voice prevalence**: the quantified language hierarchy (concept, mentions, trend, sentiment over a stated N). It leads voice sections. Anecdotes follow prevalence and never stand in for it.
3. **The umbrella**: total-scope framing before narrowing (every district, then the region, then the one school), so every later number has a visible denominator.
4. **Share-of-growth flip**: a segment small in volume and large in its share of growth, or the reverse. It shows how something small can still matter.
5. **Expected-share gap**: a segment's share of one outcome against its share of the whole, as an index, converted to what the gap costs (patients, dollars, hours). Use it when a group shows up more or less often than its size predicts.
6. **Claim against credit**: what the provider says against what users repeat back, counted on both sides. Use it wherever the provider's language and the user's language can be measured separately.
7. **The 2x2 interaction**: two factors that behave differently together than alone. Use it only when all four quadrants hold data and show their size.
8. **The honesty beat**: the strongest point AGAINST the thesis, answered directly with its size. It sits before the recommendation, because a deck that hides its weakness gets found out in the room.
9. **Leading indicator**: one small, fast-moving unit examined as an early read on the thesis, with its trajectory and its caveats (scale, recency, measurement drift).
10. **The graveyard**: what has been tried and failed here, with the measured reason, so the recommendation avoids repeating it.
11. **The watchout**: a quantified risk on the horizon that does not change the answer but bounds it.
12. **The barrier**: a factor that decides failure when absent and does little for success when present (load time, price ceilings, compliance). Naming it prevents the classic overclaim.
13. **The asymmetry test**: a liability reads negative where present and merely quiet where absent, and an asset reads positive where present. Classify each attribute before recommending that anyone lean on it.
14. **Exposure**: what share of the whole (clinics, riders, accounts) sits in the affected group, as one bar. It turns an abstract trend into "how much of us does this touch".
15. **The play**: the recommendation as two to four numbered, testable moves, each tied to the evidence slide that earned it, worded per the causality rules.
16. **Method candor**: the full scale of the work (records, sources, match rates) plus the one fragile assumption, named before anyone asks. Showing scale and admitting the assumption together makes the rest believable.
17. **The close**: restate the objective in your framing and answer it in one sentence, with the single number that carries it. Quote nothing back. The reader should recognize their question, asked more sharply.

## Ranking findings into beats

Score every candidate finding 0 to 2 on three axes: **Evidence** (how many independent sources support it), **Surprise** (does it change what the audience believes) and **Actionability** (can the reader act on it this year). A finding makes the deck only when it scores on at least two axes. Material with strong evidence and no surprise goes to the appendix. Before cutting a finding for thin evidence, widen the lens once (a longer window, an adjacent scope) to see whether the thinness is real or comes from how you cut the data.
