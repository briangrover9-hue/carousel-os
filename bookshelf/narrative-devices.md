# Narrative devices: named slide beats and analytical framings

A library of named moves for `carousel-storyboard`. These are candidates, never a template: pick the few the evidence earns, and never run the same beat twice in a row. Each beat carries across topics and segmentation choices, so you can borrow one from a slide plan that lost and use it in the one that won.

## Slide beats

1. **The shift**: the one-line change in the world that makes this deck timely. Opens a deck when the evidence supports urgency.
2. **Consumer prevalence**: the quantified language hierarchy (concept, mentions, trend, sentiment over a stated N). Leads voice sections; anecdotes follow prevalence, never substitute for it.
3. **The umbrella**: total-scope framing before narrowing (the whole market, then the category, then the territory), so every later number has a visible denominator.
4. **Share-of-growth flip**: a segment small in sales but large in growth share (or the reverse). The device that makes "small but mattering" land.
5. **Fair-share gap**: share of segment vs share of category as an index, converted to the dollars at stake. The standard over/under-indexing device.
6. **Claim vs credit**: what sellers say against what buyers repeat back, quantified on both sides. Use it wherever marketing language and customer language can be measured separately.
7. **The 2x2 interaction**: two factors that behave differently together than alone. Only when all four quadrants are populated and sized.
8. **Honesty / de-risk beat**: the strongest point AGAINST the thesis, addressed head-on with its size. Placed before the recommendation; a deck that hides its weakness gets found out in the room.
9. **Leading indicator**: one small, fast-moving entity examined as an early read on the thesis, with its trajectory and its caveats (scale, recency, ratings drift).
10. **The graveyard**: what has been tried and failed in this space, and the measured reason, so the recommendation dodges known corpses.
11. **The watchout**: a quantified risk on the horizon that does not change the answer but bounds it.
12. **Barrier, not engine**: a factor that decides failure when absent but does not drive success when present (texture, price ceilings, compliance). Prevents the classic overclaim.
13. **The asymmetry test**: a liability attribute reads negative where present and merely quiet where absent; an asset attribute reads positive where present. Classify attributes before recommending anyone lean on one.
14. **Exposure / composition**: what share of the portfolio (or category) sits in the affected territory, as one bar. Converts an abstract trend into "how much of us does this touch".
15. **The play**: the recommendation as 2-4 numbered, testable moves, each tied to the evidence slide that earned it, worded per the causality rules.
16. **Methodology candor**: the full scale of the work (corpus, records, match rates) plus the one fragile assumption named voluntarily. Flexing scale and admitting the assumption in the same breath is what makes the rest believable.
17. **The close**: restate the objective in our framing and answer it in one sentence, with the single number that carries it. Quote nothing back. The reader should recognize their question in sharper clothes.

## Ranking findings into beats

Score every candidate finding 0-2 on three axes: **Evidence** (how many independent sources or surfaces support it), **Surprise** (does it change what the audience believes), **Actionability** (can the client do something with it this year). A finding makes the deck only when it scores on at least two axes; high-evidence, zero-surprise material goes to the appendix. Before cutting a finding for thin evidence, widen the lens once (longer window, adjacent scope) to check whether the thinness is real or an artifact of the cut.
